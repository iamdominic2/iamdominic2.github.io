Clash analysis

Direct debating and collision of opposing and one’s arguments to convince that one side is better than the other. State the opponents’ arguments, state your own arguments, and (if you are doing debate competitively) state why your argument is better.
Otherwise, rebuttals can be thrown all day, and an argument would thus never end, or be thrown into a loop where neither side can demonstrate superiority over the other.
Common methods include “Even if”: Even if their point is totally true, it is still insignificant compared to the reverse side “U-turn”: Reverse their argument, where their reason for winning is actually a reason for losing “Weighing”: Compare the two sides’ values and factors and explain why one should be prioritized over the other, with a metric that is advantageous to your position. Common Metrics for weighing - Magnitude (how many people), Probability (how likely), and Urgency (how fast).
