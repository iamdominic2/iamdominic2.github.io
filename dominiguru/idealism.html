Idealism

Ideal case. I indeed won’t expect it, for it is, by definition, very rare and often impossible. I’d be amazed if it actually happened. But of course, you work towards ideals. Personally, I don’t set targets; I set ideals and work towards them as much as possible - because I don’t need the satisfaction (just a temporary chemical spike in the synapses) or external validation (ideally again, sometimes I really do need validation for my CV) from hitting a target.
The ideal, by definition, is pretty much pragmatically impossible, so I can never truly fail my expectations, as I knew that the ideal would be likely superhuman from the start.
Planning fallacy, disappointment, etc., all stem from this! Be aware of it!

The ubiquitous Idealism vs Realism clash

Idealism is the version that you prefer to be/happen.
Realism is the situation and circumstances of society, human limitations, etc.
These two likely cannot exist at the same time, as humans usually desire what they don’t have!
If ideals are not met, some people may be disappointed.
But I think ideals normally are not met; in fact, most people lose motivation over New Year's resolutions quickly. Not to lower your expectations, but it’s probably easier to set an “extreme” ideal version and work towards it, seeing how much it can be done, or set actions rather than targets.
For instance: Desire for freedom against worldly constraints. There is a lot that is wanted, but societal norms or laws restrict some of them. I prefer realism as it’s the world that needs to be cared for more.

Reality v.s. optimal

Pretty much always lower than optimal (due to pretty much guaranteed failures, like unexpected need to debug code) which may cause pessimism.
