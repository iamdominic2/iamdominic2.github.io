Pessimism

Thinking that things are more likely to turn out bad than good, or that it's safer to prepare for the worst case (Premeditation of evil).
Pessimism can contribute to loss of motivation.
Yet pessimism might be a result of learned helplessness (education system), possibly as a result of coercion and punishment (detentions)
See also: Schopenhaueristic pessimism

Be careful: to the people REALLY liking being pessimists

Most people may take pessimistic speech as a sign of depression, especially therapists who prioritize people to function well in the world, and within the world now, I consider its explicit cases where a pessimist functions well AND is well known in media extremely rare. Even if the person isn’t actually sounding depressed, most people will inevitably prefer optimism over pessimism due to societal norms and wanting to prevent their emotions from being negatively affected. Personally, I think pessimism is more realistic than irrational thinking. See: The dilemma of talking about bad things

Be careful: Don't mindlessly attack others' beliefs or values!

Telling pessimists that their pessimism is "bad" or "wrong" can hurt them! It is their choice, it is subjective, and it can be a result of determinism from past events! It's like attacking a religion: even if it has little merit (like God's existence), it's still not a good idea! Rather than forcing them to convert, tell them about brain neuroscience only if they wish to, and let them make the final decision in the end, as the brain can only really be understood by the person themselves.

Forced optimism can bring about pessimism

Forced optimism brings about pessimism due to a lack of control. The baseline state of a human is already quite optimistic. The overjustification effect will strike if you try to force someone to feel specific emotions they wouldn't naturally feel.
One very famous example is "You can do it" - prime example (also axiomatically meaningless, if you use the same logic as "CAN I BREATHE")
Inherent optimism usually gets ruined by failure or the education system - learned helplessness does not lead to optimism; optimism correlates with feelings of control: absence from coercion! Yet some schools are quite intolerant of meltdowns ("disruptive") or even the smallest signs of depression ("PR liability"), and try to suppress negative feedback. In some cases, like in my primary school, they forced students to smile. Sounds nice on paper (especially in the media), until you realize that this is totally against human biology! The sad do not get understood, which makes them even sadder.

My experience with pessimism

I was actually a pessimist for a long time and thought it was the right choice. Strangely, I think the more aware someone is, the more pessimistic they are. But I think it’s more important to notice problems in the first place, so it can work depending on mental capacity. But now, I've realized that even if it may be the most "right" choice logically, it might not be the most right choice pragmatically! Because the brain wasn't really designed to handle constant pessimism. Brain science is true. Life does not need to be fully logical. However, there still is an inner conflict with my past self, so it may take a while.

Under-estimation

Related to premeditation of evil. If you overestimate, initially you are confident, but then disappointed later on. The reverse is the case for optimism. It's a bit tricky. I prefer realism or detachment. As long as it doesn't inhibit action, e.g., by stress, whatever works is fine.

The average..

On average, being pessimistic is good assuming it does not affect your emotions that much! The reality will be better compared to what you think.

Paralysis by pessimism

Yes, worst case can paralyze brains.
However, its not the ultimate worst. I don't assume I get murdered tomorrow. Because I can't do much about it!

Pragmatic pessimism

Sometimes its better to prep for most likely scenario. Or the ones that bring about highest gains. And perhaps it's because it is easier (and more practical) to improve a bad situation to neutral than good to even good. Thus perhaps the theory that the brain prepares more for bad situations? Because it's actually true that bad situations need more fixing because higher stakes.

Detachment pessimism

Focusing only on the task, but it takes training. If there is a correlation of pessimism with readiness.

End-point disappointment

For some things not doing the thing is worse than partially doing it - you sometimes only remember the most recent thing.
