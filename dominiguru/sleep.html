Sleep

The opposite of being awake. You know what it means. I think the best definition is when you are unconscious/dreaming. The time when you are in bed (or wherever you sleep). At least, you are unaware of the "real" world. It is quite a nice thing to wake up naturally relieved. However, modern culture usually restricts awakening times by forced work and sleeping times by excessive stress.

FOR SCHOOL PEOPLE!

It is famously lacking, yet scientifically at least I think it declutters the human mind, gives it energy and reasoning abilities; I think lacking it leads to impairment; science actually shows that it leads to creativity… Waking up a teenager at 6AM is like waking an adult at 4AM. Yet the archaic school system does almost nothing about this, so sleep deprivation is a reality for many.

How to sleep

Turn lights off, don’t think too much, breathe, and actually be physically sleepy because trying to fall asleep intentionally is futile. Reduce blue light exposure, do meditation, etc.

Potential blockers of sleep

Too many thoughts or too much anxiety. Or too bright, or the room is too noisy. A possible counter is writing down stuff to free up mental capacity. Or the circadian rhythm is just not aligned, in which case it's really hard! And of course, for those who drink coffee, problems will likely arise.

Bedtime thoughts writing

Writing relieves the mind, makes one free of thoughts so it’s easier to fall asleep. Though intentionally trying to fall asleep makes it worse, as it requires the absence of stimulus.

Teenage sleeping phenomenon

It’s due to a circadian rhythm shift, yet schools don’t accommodate for this. Well, again, systematic change is difficult and will probably not be like this… That’s why, from my experience, my seniors sometimes sleep in class. Not like they won’t be able to review the stuff taught in class in their own time. It’s fine, it’s natural, don’t blame people for this, it’s biological, it happens all the time, it’s not like it would fade away from human biology anytime soon… One “hack” in the case of lack of sleep is to just take a 15-minute nap. Works well enough at school for me, usually I do so between 7:55 and 8:10, when school has not even started yet. (Technically, it isn’t even proper sleep, but again, pragmatically it suffices.)

Especially for people in the examination system: this can occur:

Bad mental health = bad sleep
Bad sleep = bad mental health
Bad sleep or bad mental health = Large hours spent studying
Large hours spent studying = bad sleep -> want to study less
Little hours spent studying = bad mental health
Uh oh! A cycle. Try detachment from it to an extent.

Sleepiness’s effects

It leads to inattention and makes one more prone to mistakes. It also leads to laziness and an inability to use the brain's full potential. This causes carelessness and sloppiness, similar to people who are highly intoxicated by ethanol. Thus, try to sleep for at least 6 hours per day.

Revenge bedtime procrastination

Where the human decides to postpone sleep to get more done.

Honestly, I occasionally fall into this, too! Studying, gaming, or watching YouTube. It can occur. Worst of all is realising that you have homework due the very next day (in which case it’s no longer about procrastination; rather, it’s about urgency). Weigh the goods and the bads before making a decision.
