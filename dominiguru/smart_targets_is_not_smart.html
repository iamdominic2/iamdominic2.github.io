SMART targets is not SMART

Specific: Targets change all the time. You have to adapt them to your current situation. Rather, for instance, in the course of programming, how I want to make a game be like will differ wildly from my initial ideas. A bigger problem may be it implies measurability in the first place. See below.
Measurable: I think this leans into school grades! After all, it's hard to measure the greatness, for instance, of a programming project.
Achievable: Technically, one can do lots of stuff. Practically, some things are up to luck. As such, achievability is a problem. A bigger concern may be the opportunity costs of it!
Relevant: Yes, but school told me to set them, which is not relevant if not for the artificial need of passing exams
Time-bound: Why the time pressure? Parkinson's law? But I thought the purpose of you wanting a target is to stop procrastination in the first place. Honestly, if you set a target for yourself, you shouldn't stress yourself out with it!

Rather the fundamental problem of targets in the first place

If the stress of having to meet the target, and the psychological consequences of not reaching a target, are worse than the joy of achieving a target, or the direction it gives, it is bad! Perhaps it's better to make them very "micro". Or go into habits instead of targets, as they can be sustained even after you reach them. Targets may make more sense for, you guessed it, school. After you get your A level A*, you can walk home happy you don't have to do that boring studying again. That is the paradox of targets!
