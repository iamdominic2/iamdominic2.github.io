Skepticism

A questioning attitude or doubt toward knowledge claims that are seen as mere beliefs. Thus, even others are questionable.
Honestly, it depends on the strength of the evidence and how long it has been accepted through common sense. Scientific evidence is usually pretty strong as it can be observed, while religious dogma is usually not as strong, as the knowledge is usually just theoretical.
Personally, I wouldn’t do the First Philosophical Skeptic: Those who deny all possibility of knowledge. Because it directly clashes with the more accepted Stoic principle of Katalepsis (Roughly comprehension in modern language). And honestly, while the First Philosophical Skeptic can be true, some things are so highly possible or near-certain, like gravity, that they pretty much can be considered universal truths…
Questioning everything leads to curiosity! (If your brain can handle it!) However, societally it makes you troubled! So you can use Google, Wikipedia, or AI to give you knowledge! Treasure what you have learned from your doubts

Political Skepticism

The truth can get distorted, especially in political events, because in passing down history, things are bent and rephrased so much that they become unreliable or misinterpreted. So, don’t thrust things that are way too old. Usually, it’s what people want you to believe, not exactly what actually happened.
Famously, religious texts like the Bible contain things pretty much unimaginable, and I think it’s false. There's no way to be sure, because no human from the past remains by definition to verify anything! Due to skepticism, not as evil, but as a pragmatic view that the past is unreliable, fragmented data, this occurs.
