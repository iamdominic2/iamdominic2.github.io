Optimism

A mental attitude of being hopeful, even in difficult situations.
The opposite of pessimism. I used to think that intelligence was great, but then later realized ignorance was bliss, and then even later realized it's just that schools guilt-tripped me into thinking anything not in the curriculum was useless and time better spent studying, but then I reversed my mind and realized I can be optimistic and smart at the same time! In reality, a dose of optimism contributes greatly to pleasure and learning, and as it is just a state of the mind, it does not imply that you are oblivious to problems (rather, optimistic people may be socially aware and selectively decide not to talk about problems to avoid ruining a good mood.)

Inherent optimism vs forced optimism

Forced optimism brings about pessimism - because of lack of control. The baseline state of a human is already quite optimistic. The overjustification effect will strike if you try to force someone to feel specific emotions they wouldn't naturally feel.
"You can do it" - prime example (also axiomatically meaningless, if you use the same logic as "You can theoretically win the lottery.)
Inherent optimism usually gets ruined by failure or the education system - learned helplessness does not lead to optimism; optimism correlates with feelings of control, i.e. freedom, i.e. absence from coercion!

Childhood optimism illusion

This is evolutionary, it might be that, factually, babies are pretty much powerless; they can’t even walk, talk, or choose what to eat. So if there was nothing else working for them, babies might just choose not to breathe and die.
Children are born with a high optimism bias as a survival mechanism; alternatively, because they have no agency, they are blissfully unaware of the adult world too.
I was one of the few children to even doubt whether happiness and friends are good, as I could see lots of pain in Chinese even when I was in primary school.
On the other hand, I think this theory can also be learned helplessness from school or work, where agency is also suppressed; over time, the person becomes neutral and obedient: subjectivity and "disruptions" not being valued; most would end up that way.
