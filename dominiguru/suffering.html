Suffering

The state of pain, or undesired things. Like school cutting off sleep, which is one of the most important things to a pleasant life! This is what utilitarianism, hedonism, darwinism, and many others try to minimize. In fact, this may be one of the most hated things in human history ever.

Human suffering

Existence is basically a swing between pain and boredom (Schopenhauerism. Thus, humans will eternally suffer for as long as they are alive, even starting from childbirth, as the child cannot express his or her ideas in words, cannot walk, and cannot speak… However, some forms of human suffering are indeed worse than others; being tortured or held captive is pretty high in suffering, while other things like a small grape falling onto the floor might be lower. The greatest forms of suffering, like natural disasters, are usually helped out.
It’s inevitable, as long as they exist. However, the severity of suffering depends on a whole lot of factors, including living conditions, finances/assets, cognitive thinking skills, and problem-solving skills. Still, it exists, like people existing in places where food and education are extremely scarce and rare.

Is recognition of problems better than the suffering of the fear of the unknown?

I think so, yet most people have fear in discovery, as it can be against what they previously thought of; this is confirmation bias.

Suffering and pleasure.

Life is all suffering

Thus, the goal of life is to eliminate suffering.

The three ways to avoid suffering are all flawed.

1. Actually not having them means no will to do things -> boredom (which to the human brain… is indeed another problem.) Plus, it's pretty much impossible to be free of suffering.

2. Ignorance of suffering, in this case, most problems compound over time. Examples:

2a. The problem of lack of oxygen arrives every 10 seconds or so, yet if you stop breathing, it quickly becomes an expensive and dangerous medical issue

2b. Societal views of maturity. eg. With age, some actions like “childish behavior”, for instance, tomfoolery, are seen as less and less acceptable. (Even though brains do mature, isn’t this age restriction a bias/stereotype? But I think it’s fine because it’s somewhat accurate and the image of an adult doing childish things is hard to imagine.)
3. The counterargument to saying “living itself is a problem, thus you have to get rid of it” is that getting rid of the will to live is an even greater problem (and is not possible within evolutionary principles), and biologically, and by the laws of evolution, and by social conditioning, it is indeed pretty hard to do.

Unsolvable suffering

Some problems of suffering, like the noise within school caused by others, or corrupt politicians like Kim Jong Un. Usually, the sufferings that are not solvable are indeed caused by other people with much higher authority than me, or the volume is just much higher than one can control. And the brain usually panics when it realizes some things are simply not possible to solve by itself (groups can work, but convincing people, especially authority figures, to change, is nearly impossible.)

Pain > Pleasure “Proof”

In life, fundamentally, some say pain is greater than pleasure. But people try not to admit that. They expect that to not be true, chase pleasure, and then fall in the face of pain and fail their logic

Compare the feelings of an animal that is eating another with those of the animal that is being eaten.

There is more pain than pleasure, as humans create more pain than pleasure when they kill an organism (organism, plant feels pain? Regardless, animals certainly feel pain).

Alternatively, an organism eating the same species, pain of death probably greater than the pleasure of the survivor eating the dead, means a bit of pleasure from temporarily mitigating the problem of hunger, but not for humans.

Yet some say that how we measure suffering and pleasure is merely subjective, and due to darwinism and the will to live, pain is more noticeable than joy! Objectively, there may not be a way to classify.
