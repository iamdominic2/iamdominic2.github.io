Paradox

Something that seems another way at first glance, or contradicts itself, logically making it make a bit of sense, and then later you get confused and end up like, "What? This is absurd, yet is somehow True!"

Funeral paradox

A dead person is simply in a state of non-existence. Even if you pray, even if you burn money, or give them gifts, it is all futile as the corpse would not come back to life; it has already undergone death. Thus, it could be a waste of time and money and energy just because it’s a tradition; you should have said your last farewells when he or she was still alive! It’s just that the feeling of grief overwrites most humans’ thinking systems to the point where cannibalism is illegal even though technically it’s a free meat resource (however, this is likely due to health reasons), and methods to bury the dead “properly” are enforced with the threat of legal punishment otherwise (but this is also a health issue). It is mainly said to be just social synchronization because everyone else is doing the same thing. Respecting the dead? The corpse cannot feel respected as they have a lack of sensory input.

Paradox of listening

This does not indicate or show whether the listener is actually "listening" or not.
On the most biological level, technically, only the ears are needed, even if the sound is from different directions.
However, if I'm looking away, people think I'm not listening. Yet, by axiomatic nature, listening is with ears, so practically, if you live by the minimum requirement, your eyes do not need to be focused on the speaker.
Society is wrong! Rather arbitrary "respect", even if you forget what you hear soon after. Technically speaking, looking at the speaker is of little importance of how important the content is. More likely, it reflects the social position of, or how "close" you are (for instance, as a friend) to the speaker himself or herself. Teachers scold; parents scold. With basic logic, I could already see this as a fallacy. Rather, focus is more important, and society likes eye contact, so pragmatically you may still have to do so against people who do not seem to want to listen to such logic. However, I do see societally that in large groups, there needs to be a good indicator of who one is speaking to, and whether one puts the attention to another's face is evolutionarily quite significant at giving an implied message that you want to listen to the speaker.
