Correlation is not necessarily causation

This is a common bias or fallacy. It refers to what directly causes something versus what increases/decreases as another thing increases/decreases (same way)
Just because B follows A in time, B is not necessarily the result of A.
Sometimes, these can be tricky to pinpoint!!

Examples

1) Summer: Ice Cream sales rise, but drowning incidents also rise: Ice cream doesn’t cause drowning.
2) Claim: "Schools develop Critical Thinking Skills because higher age students can think better." (Correlation)
Reality: Prefrontal Cortex development can happen even in the absence of the existence of school. (Not a causation)
3) "Social media causes depression more than school." While there is a correlation, it is not necessarily the biggest causator. This is because in summer, logically, you should get more social media time, but depression rates are nearly halved (Peter Gray)! Rather, social media may lead to people having unrealistic expectations or attention spans, which I would say was inevitable, given that schools don't entertain students, other companies developed and led to this: the "innovator" usually wins.
Rather, students with little coping ability for the education system and are depressed as a result for it, may spend more time on social media, coping by doing what they want!!
