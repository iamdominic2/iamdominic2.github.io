Depressive realism

The idea that being pessimistic makes you more accurate to reality, and also as a result makes "better" judgments. It suggests mildly depressed individuals perceive reality more accurately than “normal” because “normal” is usually a bit more optimistic than reality would suggest. It is seen as a way for more objective perceptions, but too much depression can be bad.
I had this view for a long time, possibly due to some random people claiming "You can do it!" even when doubtful, making me feel like society was in an illusion of optimism. It was mainly due to a lack of freedom in the education system, contributing to my sense that I have little power to change the "dark" reality I perceived, and doubted for so long. However, I realized that their goal wasn't scientific or statistical truth! (In fact, daily communication rarely is that way)

Drawbacks

I tried to deny, but eventually realized the obvious: even if logical, it can be bad for your psychology! But, the drawback of depressive realism is that THINKING realistically, e.g., what is likely to happen, might make one avoid venturing into new stuff. EG "The most likely food I can enjoy without drawbacks is just the ones I constantly eat right now." In reality, though this is technically true, you would not be satisfying the brain's novelty-seeking needs.

Self Depreciation

One technique of depressive realism. Making the self sound really bad, so you might actually be comparatively better. Or it could be socially more acceptable, but sociology may have other things to say about it, like it just being a coping mechanism, and it making you seem weak.
