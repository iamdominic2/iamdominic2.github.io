Stoicism

An ancient Greek philosophy that seems to be resurging recently.

Stoicism is where you focus on what is in control, and accept that within the environment, there will inevitably be external influences, whether fair or unfair, ethical, moral, or not; such things are bound to happen, and you shouldn't let things that he or she cannot control affect you as far as possible. The Premeditation of Evil helps with that. Otherwise, detached, calm acceptance of things as they are without attempts to resist or change them can work.

Don't let things like other people or your mood influence actions or your feelings about life, to the best of your ability.

This might be the best philosophy for dealing with adversity just like its creator Marcus Aurelius... but this assumes you can actually practice this philosophy strongly. Of course it would be a survivorship bias where the creator is the most known due to him by definition doing it to his definition. It's... hard! Very hard! To do this!

Stoicism & Education system

On the way, you may meet some strong-willed people, immense suffering, and other obstacles, but this teaches you to basically not let such things impact your thinking and doing capacities, because though you cannot control other people, you can control your reaction and future (mitigating) actions.
A common misconception is having no reactions or emotions, like grey rocking. However, you can absolutely have reactions (otherwise you won't be able to communicate properly), it's just about reducing the impact of your emotions on your internal state. Paradoxically, I think the education system itself is a big cause for Stoicism for the people who know it! Because (hopefully) they would be able to realize:
Because they did not control the education system, and the system itself is highly restrictive, it is not indicative of their "real" or "unrestricted" virtue, so they can just detach! No emotions!

Prohairesis

The rational capacity to decide how we respond to external impressions. It represents the only thing truly under our control, distinguishing us as people who can choose virtue over passion, regardless of external circumstances!

Past-Future Stoicism

Radical acceptance of the past, because this is the sure thing you cannot change (which would make it not recommended to focus on by the rules of Stoicism itself); and the future is also (mostly) uncontrollable right now by definition. So it's what now that can be controlled...

Actionable Stoicism

The next step can have some pragmatism, in fact, it's quite logical: the more control you have over something, the more agency exists, and the more positive change that can be done to the particular thing in your control, so do what you can!

Benefits

In such a world, there is indeed little to no control over what people like authority figures do, like the ridiculousness of Donald Trump, or if you have little authority, like a student at school, it will inevitably be the case that there is a lot that is not in your control. It helps save your mental energy for things that you deem have more value. If done correctly, you will likely feel a sense of peace about what is happening around you. Stoicism can also help with emotional regulation if done well.

Drawbacks

It's indeed tiring. Even though it sounds easy to not care at first glance, from my viewpoint, it actually takes so much practice that it makes me get mad at certain things and start reacting. The most unfair situations are like false accusations, for instance, when I am suspected by a teacher of having done something wrong, because they hold absolute power over me. And also, it might seem cold at first glance... but it's actually fine, you can pragmatically "fake" your emotions towards others because your internal state, by axiomatic definition, is hidden from others and can, and will be different from what you show "outside", which is what I would call a social mask. Still, take it with a pragmatic grain of salt.

BTW, my stoic expectations

I have little way of knowing what others do anymore because I have zero agency over others’ decision-making. The only thing left I can reasonably expect is that they don’t do intentional, targeted harm, like an aggravated assault towards me, as there usually isn’t enough motivation to break the law just to injure me physically and bring about anger.

Amor fati

Where you detach from the actual outcome, when it is up to fate, or luck. You can't control this, so the usual stoic practices of caring about only what is in your control applies here.

The Emotional vs The Stoic

"Emotionalism" is basically having lots of emotions, like laughing, crying, anger, happiness…
Being a stoic is basically not feeling much, not being impacted much by external people and such. I prefer to be stoic, as emotions distort logical thinking, and logic is usually a more reliable way of thinking than emotional “reasoning”. However, not showing emotions can be socially cold to people. And may be psychologically almost impossible. Yet I see the benefits of being stable! So stoicism is great!

Altruistic stoicism: possibly my new philosophy!

When you cannot change the past, which was shaped by determinism - the same applies to others, so how others are is due to their surrounding nature, nurturing, so they can't change the past of what they have done, their past of e.g. child abuse they suffered through; neither can they really control much! When this happens, you will accept people much more than before.

Tricky: partially in control

Sometimes it's not that clear - you can control some but not other aspects of an activity; for instance, exam grades are affected by your studies.
However, you can control your studies, even if you cannot exactly control the exam result due to the chaotic reality of noise. So it's not like "Help! I can't do ANYTHING to help my grades!" because it's a bit of cause-and-effect.

Beginner's mistake

When you think you are a Stoic yet still flinch from negativity, the first fallacy that I fell into TOO is that I am not stoic enough. Rather, it's most likely you are just a beginner, and by definition, the "used-to-ness" of detachment is the weakest. It's still a very valuable philosophy in the uncontrollable past and others' behaviors, so with most things, it needs some training to have its effects shown.

What it ignores

You do usually have to do something if the world is acting unjustly towards you. Others are technically not that much in your control - but still a little bit of power might exist. It ignores the fact that people facing bad things often have a less good life.

Toxic stoicism

BEWARE! You might expect to feel nothing, or others to feel nothing. Still, the biological impulse that is sometimes encounterable is that you feel pain when you do! And this applies to most negative emotions in general... The truth is that if it hurts, it DOES hurt by definition.

The trap -> likely to repeat if not broken. Forever!
