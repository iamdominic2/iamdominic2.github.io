Detachment

Not caring much. Barely. State of being (mentally) disconnected.
It's related to asceticism and buddhism, and also stoicism (for things you cannot control).
Detachment is a state in which a person overcomes their emotional attachment to or desire for things, people, or worldly concerns and thus attains a heightened perspective.
Detach from the need to "stay informed"! If you cannot help the world, it only leads to learned helplessness
Detach from "doomscrolling" at the cost of other tasks! If you do so instead of a higher priority, it makes you miserable at the end of it
Detach from socializing (some forms)? Because it can be higher entropy?
If you are, perhaps, in the examination system, detachment- not caring about the rest of the world MAY allow you to focus only on the task, which makes it more pragmatic! This is the value. It takes training. Deep breathing could help. However, the payoffs are immense if you use it mainly in situations that are likely to cause stress.

Its paradox

Strictly speaking, if one is a biological entity, total detachment is a paradox. A collection of chemical reactions designed to make you "care" about, e., Breathing cannot ignore itself. If you exist within school, some school stuff like homework will still impact you (and your time/energy). Such impossible standards are naturally impossible. However, detachment is likely still a spectrum, and if you can already relieve yourself from a few negative emotions, it is fine already.

Paradox #2

Even though, as said before, you of course cannot detach fully, you might actually somehow have better performance when somewhat detached. When you detach from caring about the outcome itself, for some things like exams (which usually bring about more stress than optimum if you REALLY "care" about them beyond the fact it's a way to university), you might actually do better due to less resistance from your brain, which may have slightly less interference on sleep, daily life, and such.
